## How official Stockfish handles history

The UCI position command supplies the initial position and the played moves. The engine stores a StateInfo for every replayed move, and its worker attaches the latest state to the earlier history when starting a search. A FEN records the current board, rights and clocks. It cannot reconstruct prior occurrences. Earlier history than the supplied starting position is unavailable. [Engine::set_position](https://github.com/official-stockfish/Stockfish/blob/1819b9ea37588be8adb044179fb54512f87c8249/src/engine.cpp#L195) · [ThreadPool::start_thinking](https://github.com/official-stockfish/Stockfish/blob/1819b9ea37588be8adb044179fb54512f87c8249/src/thread.cpp#L320)

For a played move, the position key incorporates side to move, castling rights and a legally available en passant opportunity. An equal-square arrangement can therefore have different repetition rights. The engine scans backward at two-ply intervals within the reversible and null-move horizon. It verifies the key and packed piece placement. The repetition field is positive for the distance to the second occurrence, negative upon the third and subsequent occurrences, and zero with no match. At ply one after a legal root move, `is_repetition(1)` identifies a third or later occurrence in the supplied history. At a deeper search node, the predicate can also recognize repeated positions strictly inside the search as draws. The patch uses the ply-one predicate for the actual moves compared. Upcoming repetition can improve the lower bound in the main search away from the root and in quiescence. An immediate search draw normally gets a small node-dependent jitter (−1 or +1 internal evaluation unit) in the main search and a zero score in quiescence. This preserves a slight move-order dependence in the experiment. Stockfish's UCI WDL is a material-conditioned statistical model. For two **identical internal scores at the same root**, the model maps to exactly the same reported win/draw/loss triplet. A finite search can still misjudge a continuation. An available threefold can secure a draw that equal model estimates cannot guarantee after another move. ## Candidate decision

At full-strength SinglePV the candidate first checks for two or more allowed root moves, an available repetition, no existing third occurrence of the current position, a halfmove clock below 99, and a root outside the Syzygy tablebase regime. Each depth begins with the usual single PV. Only when the ordinary first PV actually leads to a third occurrence and its unbounded search score is at least zero does the engine spend time on a second PV. After the second search completes, a still-leading repetition can be displaced by the explicit tie-break only if the second PV has the same internal score, carries neither bound flag, and does not itself immediately repeat. A strictly higher score continues to win by normal ordering. A worse alternative, a failed bound, a forced move or an interrupted comparison cannot trigger the explicit swap. `searchmoves` restrictions and score jitter remain active. [Eligibility and position replay](https://github.com/ShadyF4RR/Stockfish/blob/293da879bd27da174728cda7b5b46d8011f2a3ac/src/search.cpp#L332) · [Root comparison](https://github.com/ShadyF4RR/Stockfish/blob/293da879bd27da174728cda7b5b46d8011f2a3ac/src/search.cpp#L527)

This tests immediate root choices only. A subsequent forced repetition, an opponent's prospective claim or a refutation beyond the searched horizon remains possible. The second PV can use clock time and perturb search history, move ordering and later scores even when the explicit switch never fires. A strong test must compare the same clock, inspect full histories, report practical results and test ordinary positions. Internal scores and NPS alone cannot settle strength.

## Claims, automatic draws and mate

Under the FIDE Laws of Chess a player can claim a draw on three occurrences, including by indicating a legal move that would produce the third, or after 50 moves by each player without a capture or pawn move. Five occurrences or 75 such moves by each player end the game automatically. Checkmate on the last move takes precedence over the 75-move automatic draw. Stalemate and dead positions also end the game. A match controller must decide the actual game result and retain the complete game history. [FIDE Laws, articles 5, 9.2, 9.3 and 9.6](https://handbook.fide.com/chapter/E012023)

The upstream search treats an eligible 50-move claim as a draw at a non-root node. At a halfmove count over 99 it first checks for checkmate when the king is in check. The ordinary no-move search path distinguishes mate and stalemate. Upstream uses the repetition marker for third and later occurrences without a separate fivefold count, and its search has no distinct 150-halfmove adjudicator. UCI may return a legal move if asked to search a claimable or automatically drawn root; a GUI or arbiter ends that game. The experiment changes neither rule detection nor game adjudication. It disables its comparison before a root move can first reach 100 halfmoves. At a root already on its third or later occurrence, including its fifth, its comparison is also disabled.
